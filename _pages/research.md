# Research

My research uses statistical and computational approaches to modern and ancient genomes to understand human evolutionary history, especially how selection has shaped variation in traits like pigmentation.

<div class="research-grid">

<div class="research-card">
<img src="{{ '/images/research/selection.svg' | relative_url }}" class="research-thumb" width="400" height="200" alt="Allele frequency trajectories over time">
<div class="research-body">
<h2 class="research-title">Selection and Demography from Ancient DNA Time Series</h2>
<p class="research-desc">In the <a href="https://nielsen-lab.github.io/">Nielsen Lab</a> at UC Berkeley, I am developing and optimizing inference under a structured Wright&#8211;Fisher model that jointly estimates selection coefficients, migration rates, and effective population sizes from serial allele-frequency time series across multiple structured populations. I built an HPC pipeline applying the method to the Allen Ancient DNA Resource 2M SNP panel to study the evolution of pigmentation and lactase persistence in ancient Eurasian populations.</p>

<div class="research-body">
<h2 class="research-title">Pigmentation Genetics in Archaic Hominins</h2>
<p class="research-desc">My honors thesis with Dr. Tina Lasisi evaluated whether pigmentation can be reliably predicted from Neanderthal and Denisovan genomes. Using public data from the Simons Genome Diversity Project and the Max Planck Institute for Evolutionary Anthropology, I assessed sequencing depth, missingness, and genotype reliability in low-coverage archaic genomes and projected archaic samples onto genetic PCAs of modern humans. The <a href="https://doi.org/10.7302/dspace/30029">thesis</a> is available on Deep Blue, the analyses are on the <a href="https://lilyheald.github.io/PAINT/">project site</a>, and a manuscript is in preparation.</p>
</div>
</div>

<div class="research-body">
<h2 class="research-title">Human Pigmentation and Hair Morphology</h2>
<p class="research-desc">In the <a href="https://www.lasisilab.com/">Lasisi Lab</a>, I analyzed a high-dimensional dataset of genetic, survey, and skin-reflectance measures to test the assumptions behind constitutive and facultative pigmentation metrics, and built a reference database of pigmentation-associated genes and SNPs. I also co-authored a review of classical and genomic research on human pigmentation and hair morphology, now under review at <em>Trends in Genetics</em>.</p>
</div>
</div>

<div class="research-card">
<img src="{{ '/images/research/isotopes.svg' | relative_url }}" class="research-thumb" width="400" height="200" alt="Sequential enamel isotope profiles">
<div class="research-body">
<h2 class="research-title">Zooarchaeology and Stable Isotopes</h2>
<p class="research-desc">With the Ancient Protein and Isotope Laboratory Group, I sequentially sampled enamel from 21 sheep, goat, and cattle molars to reconstruct animal diet and mobility as proxies for economic specialization and herd management in the Early Bronze Age southern Levant. I wrote the R package <a href="https://github.com/lilyheald/isotopeTools">isotopeTools</a> to automate this analysis.</p>
